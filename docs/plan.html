UrbanTwin: Market-Sim

Group implementation plan · Claude Impact Lab #2, Track 2 · Fraunhofer IIS Nürnberg · 7-hour build (13:00–20:00) · 4 people

1. What we ship

A browser decision tool that answers "where should Nuremberg's vegetable market go?". The tool does not start from two fixed sites. It scans the inner city for candidate spaces, filters out the unsuitable ones, ranks the rest, and runs a persona-based pedestrian and delivery simulation on the shortlist. The official candidates (Hauptmarkt, Lorenzkirche, former Kaufhof) always appear as benchmarks. Claude explains the computed result, and a what-if panel recalculates the result under different conditions and priorities.

Two principles shape the design:

  • Deliveries are part of the model. A site with high footfall that a 3.5-ton van cannot reach at 05:30 fails. Delivery access is a hard filter in step 2 and a score factor in step 6.
  • Stakeholders disagree, and the tool shows it. The MarketScore is a weighted sum, but the screen always shows the four persona scores beside it. A high total cannot hide a group that loses badly.

The deliverables are:

  • the live app,
  • a 45–60 second recorded simulation video (the pitch opener and the backup if the network fails),
  • a 2-minute pitch.

3. Prior work and what we take from it

WorkWhat it showsWhat we adopt
Park et al., Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior (Stanford/Google, arXiv:2304.03442)LLM agents with profiles, memory, reflection and daily plans produce believable behavior in a sandbox town.Persona profiles with goals, budgets and daily schedules. We skip memory streams: 100 agents × 15 ticks is too slow and costly for a live demo.
Verma et al., Generative agents in the streets: exploring LLMs in collecting urban perceptions (arXiv:2312.13126)LLM agents with personas judge street environments from Street View imagery and OSM. The paper is about perception, not route choice.Persona-specific judgments of a site ("too crowded for my rollator"), written by Claude from the computed metrics
Zheng et al., Urban planning in the era of large language models (MIT Senseable City Lab, Nature Computational Science, 2025)A conceptualize → generate → evaluate loop in which synthetic residents use competing designs and the designs are scored on equity, travel distance and facility use.Evaluating competing sites with synthetic residents, using equity and travel distance as criteria (our Fairness and Accessibility)
Zhou et al., LiPUP-MA: a residential experience-centric multi-agent framework for living-in-the-loop participatory urban planning (arXiv:2412.20505), the cyclical "plan, live, judge" line of workPlanner agents propose, resident agents "live" in the plan and complain, and the plan is revised in cycles.The propose → simulate → judge loop: Claude proposes a mitigation, the engine reruns, and the personas judge the result
Piao et al., AgentSociety (arXiv:2502.08691) and Yan et al., OpenCity (arXiv:2410.21286)Thousands of LLM agents run in GIS environments to study mobility and policy responses. Scaling them requires batching and grouping agents.The OpenCity idea of grouping agents into archetypes: one LLM call per persona, not per agent
When Plausible Is Not Realistic: Evaluating Human Mobility in LLM-Based Urban Simulation (arXiv:2606.13835)LLM-generated mobility can look plausible and still fail to match real movement statistics.The reason our movement is rule-based and seeded, and footfall is labeled "modeled exposure"

Our design position (hybrid): deterministic Monte Carlo agents decide where people walk and drive. This part is fast, reproducible and testable. Claude decides what it means. It writes persona judgments, mediates trade-offs, and proposes mitigations that the engine then verifies. This combines the believability of generative agents with the reliability that a planning tool needs, and it puts us next to published work instead of reinventing it.

The "Awesome Urban LLM Agents" list (github.com/usail-hkust/Awesome-Urban-LLM-Agents) collects further work. We could not find a paper titled exactly "Planning, Living, and Judging", so we cite LiPUP-MA for the cyclical approach. Verify the titles before quoting them on slides.

  • Everything runs in the browser except Claude. Ranking and simulation are pure, seeded TypeScript functions, so a what-if recalculation takes about a second and works offline.
  • Weights apply after simulation. The simulation produces raw indicators once per site and scenario. Moving a weight slider only recomputes the weighted sum, so the ranking updates instantly.
  • One small server route holds the Anthropic key and calls Claude. The key never reaches the browser.
  • Claude explains the numbers and never invents them. The engine computes every number, and Claude interprets the trade-offs.

7. Roles

A · Geodata, Discovery & Ranking

Owns: prep/, public/data/, src/rank/, video recording

  • Discover: extract about 15–25 candidates from OSM: place=square, pedestrian areas above 800 m², large vacant ground floors. Add the 3 benchmarks.
  • Indicators per candidate: open area (after subtracting LoD2 buildings), transit frequency from GTFS stops within 300 m, population within 800 m from Zensus, retail and attraction POIs within 400 m, slope
  • Filter: area below 800 m², no transit stop within 400 m, or no van route to a loading point within 80 m (using vehicle-access edges and bollards) → rejected, with a stated reason
  • Rank: normalized indicator score, top 3–5 plus benchmarks → shortlist
  • graph.json (edges with steps, bollard, slope, vehicleAllowed) and population.json
  • After 16:30: the Playwright script that records the video

Done when: the candidate map shows rejected sites greyed out with reasons, the shortlist is defensible, and Lorenzkirche gets flagged for delivery access.

B · Pedestrian & Delivery Twin

Owns: src/sim/ (pure TypeScript, Vitest)

  • Seeded RNG and agent origins drawn from real sources: U-Bahn and tram arrivals (GTFS), Zensus residential cells, shopping streets, attractions
  • Persona mix: seniors (share taken from the Zensus 65+ grid), commuters, shoppers and tourists, vendor vans at 05:30–07:00
  • Dijkstra with per-persona cost: seniors avoid steps, slope over 6% and cobblestones; vans need vehicle edges; rain raises the cost of unsheltered edges
  • 30-second ticks: arrive → route → stall visits → leave, with drop-outs when patience or walking budget runs out
  • Output: the five criteria, persona scores, stall exposure, bottlenecks, trips, heat
  • Scenario switches: rainy day, and a Christmas market that blocks the Hauptmarkt and doubles tourist agents

Done when: one site and scenario runs in under 1.5 s, the same seed gives the same result, and the shortlist sites produce clearly different persona profiles.

C · Map, Ranking & What-if UI

Owns: src/ui/

  • deck.gl over MapLibre: DOP20 base, extruded LoD2, candidate polygons colored by rank (rejected ones greyed out with a tooltip reason)
  • Simulation view: TripsLayer agents by persona, exposure HeatmapLayer, bottleneck markers, time slider
  • Ranking table and MarketScore bars that reorder live as the five weight sliders move, plus presets
  • What-if bar: Sunny / Rainy / Christmas market
  • Persona cards, the Claude brief panel, "Export council brief"

Done when: moving a weight reorders the ranking in under 100 ms and the map stays at 60 fps with 100 trails.

D · Scoring, Claude & Pitch

Owns: src/score/, server/brief.ts, prompts, the pitch deck

  • score.ts: normalization, MarketScore, fairness Gini, and a minimum-persona guard that flags any persona below 40
  • /api/brief: sends the ranking, criteria and active weights to claude-sonnet-5-5 with a forced write_recommendation tool (Brief schema). Claude must cite engine numbers and compare against at least two alternatives.
  • Persona judgments: one Claude call per persona (not per agent) turns that persona's metrics into a first-person verdict for the cards
  • Propose → simulate → judge loop: Claude proposes a mitigation (a kiosk, a delivery window, a stall layout), B's engine reruns, and the personas judge again
  • Re-brief on every what-if, with a short "what changed and why" delta
  • Cached briefs per scenario as an offline fallback; key in .env
  • Integration owner from 17:00; pitch and Method slide

Done when: every number in the brief matches the input, the brief arrives in under 6 s, and the cached fallback works offline.

8. Scoring model

MarketScore = 0.30·Accessibility + 0.25·Footfall + 0.20·Fairness + 0.15·LocalBusiness + 0.10·Walkability (default weights; adjustable, always normalized to sum to 1)

Criterion (0–100)DefinitionSource
AccessibilityShare of simulated visitors, weighted by persona, who reach the market within their walking budget. Seniors count only if their route has no steps and stays within 200 m of an elevator or stop. Vendor van access is a hard requirement.Simulation + GTFS
FootfallModeled visitor exposure per Saturday. This is a prediction and is not validated against counts.Simulation
FairnessStall-to-stall evenness: 100 × (1 − Gini of stallExposure). A market where the corner stalls get all the visitors scores low.Simulation
Local businessExtra footfall past existing shop frontages within 200 m, minus blocked frontage and waste exposureSimulation + OSM shops
WalkabilityMean route slope, steps, cobblestones and crossing count from visitor originsGraph + DGM1

Weight presets for the what-if panel: Accessibility first (0.45/0.20/0.15/0.10/0.10), Trader fairness first (0.20/0.20/0.40/0.10/0.10), Local business first (0.20/0.25/0.10/0.35/0.10).

11. Demo script (2 minutes)

  1. 0:00, video: the camera flies into the 3D Altstadt. Caption: "The market has to move. Where?"
  2. 0:15, Discover and filter: about 20 candidates appear, and the filter greys out most of them with reasons ("no van access", "too small").
  3. 0:35, Simulate: the shortlist plus the benchmarks run. Agents stream from U-Bahn exits and homes, vans arrive at 05:30, and the heatmap shows exposure per stall.
  4. 1:00, Score: the ranking appears with persona cards. Lorenzkirche wins on footfall and fails on deliveries.
  5. 1:15, Claude: "Site B is recommended because… Site C is better at X but worse at Y." Claude proposes a mitigation for the group that loses. The engine reruns it, and the persona cards update their verdicts.
  6. 1:35, What-if: switch to Rainy, then to "Trader fairness first". The ranking reorders live, and Claude explains what changed.
  7. 1:55, Export the council brief.
